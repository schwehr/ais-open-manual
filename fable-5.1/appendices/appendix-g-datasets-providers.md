## G.1 Open-access and government datasets

Open-access and sovereign government datasets form the bedrock of empirical maritime research, spatial ocean planning, safety auditing, and environmental modeling. These datasets originate from national coastal base station networks conforming to IEC 62320-1 ([Chapter 20](../chapters/ch20-architecture-and-station-classes.md)), regional maritime information federations, or non-governmental public-interest research organizations.

While statutory open data mandates (such as the US Open Government Data Act or the EU Open Data Directive) ensure free public re-use, collection architectures differ sharply in their temporal granularity, spatial bounds, latency, and redaction policies. ## G.2 Commercial providers and market consolidation

Commercial providers operate global surveillance networks that bridge the territorial limits of national shore networks. They rely on two foundational architectures: spaceborne Low Earth Orbit (LEO) microsatellite constellations equipped with software-defined radio payloads ([Chapter 39](../chapters/ch39-satellite-ais.md)), and massive distributed crowdsourcing grids maintained by coastal volunteers ([Chapter 41](../chapters/ch41-networks-and-providers.md)).

Between 2021 and 2026, private equity acquisition and corporate consolidation dramatically reshaped the commercial vendor landscape. Independent crowdsourced platforms (such as MarineTraffic and FleetMon) and satellite constellation operators (such as exactEarth and Spire Maritime) were aggregated under consolidated data conglomerates (notably Kpler). Concurrently, specialized analytics firms developed closed machine learning platforms that ingest raw multi-provider telemetry to detect deceptive shipping practices, sanctions evasion, and dark fleet transshipments.

## G.3 Ingest protocols, data models, and schemas

The technical mechanisms for ingesting and processing AIS data depend on whether the consumer interacts with raw bitstreams, transport-encapsulated NMEA sentences, pre-decoded tabular archives, or event-driven cloud message queues ([Chapter 26](../chapters/ch26-interfaces-and-logging.md), [Chapter 41](../chapters/ch41-networks-and-providers.md)).

Table G.3 provides a side-by-side engineering comparison of the primary schemas, field conventions, timestamp semantics, coordinate encodings, and parsing quirks encountered across the major public and commercial AIS feeds.

### Table G.3: Comparison of AIS Data Ingestion Schemas and Protocol Specifications

| Dataset / Interface | Transport Mechanism | Timestamp Encoding & Clock Semantics | Coordinate System & Number Formatting | Core Dynamic & Static Fields Exposed | Unique Idiosyncrasies & Parsing Traps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Raw NMEA 0183 (`!AIVDM`)** | UDP datagrams, TCP sockets, RS-422 serial | No native UTC date; EPFS seconds field (`0–59`, `60`=unavailable) in payload | 6-bit ASCII compressed integers; scaled by $10^{-4}$ minutes (1/10,000 min) | Message Type, MMSI, Nav Status, ROT, SOG, Longitude, Latitude, COG, Heading, Static Dimensions, Call Sign, IMO | Multi-sentence fragmentation (`!AIVDM,2,1,...`); requires 6-bit ASCII unpacking, bit-level sign extension, and CRC XOR verification |
| **IEC 62320-1 Base Station Stream** | Raw TCP socket (e.g. Kystverket `153.44.253.27:5631`) | TAG blocks prepend sentences: `\c:1774825200*hh\` (Unix epoch second) or `\c:1774825200350*hh\` (millisecond) | Encapsulated in standard `!AIVDM` payload | Prepend metadata: Station ID (`\s:b0021\`), reception epoch (`\c:`), line count (`\n:`) | Stream consumers must parse leading TAG blocks before passing encapsulated `!AIVDM` payload to standard NMEA decoders |

| **Marine Cadastre Parquet / GeoParquet (2025+)** | Cloud object storage (S3 / HTTPS) | `base_date_time` stored as native 64-bit integer timestamp (`TIMESTAMP_NTZ` or UTC microsecond) | WGS84 decimal degrees (lower-case column names `longitude`, `latitude`); geometry encoded as WKB geometry column | `mmsi`, `base_date_time`, `longitude`, `latitude`, `sog`, `cog`, `heading`, `vessel_name`, `imo`, `call_sign`, `vessel_type`, `status`, `length`, `width`, `draft`, `cargo`, `transceiver` | All column names converted to snake_case; numeric bounds enforced (`sog` 0–99.9 kn, `heading` 0–359°); parquet dictionary encoding enables zero-copy vectorized filtering |

Metadata topic: `name`, `callsign`, `imo`, `draft`, `destination`, `eta`, `shipType` | Inconsistent timestamp units across topics (seconds vs milliseconds); dynamic and static messages published asynchronously on separate MQTT topic branches |
