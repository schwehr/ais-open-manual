# Appendix B — Standards register

This register collects the international conventions, performance standards, radio recommendations, industrial type-approval test standards, interface specifications, and operational guidelines that govern the Automatic Identification System (AIS) and its direct extensions. For an architectural explanation of how these tiers interlock, see [Chapter 15](../chapters/ch15-standards-that-define-ais.md); for the statutory carriage mandates and treaties, see [Chapter 16](../chapters/ch16-laws-and-treaties.md); and for institutional responsibilities, see [Chapter 14](../chapters/ch14-key-organizations.md) and [Appendix J](../appendices/appendix-j-organizations.md).

The standards are grouped into eight structural domains:
1. **Statutory and Operational Performance (IMO):** Foundational carriage mandates under SOLAS Chapter V, operational use guidelines, installation circulars, and application-specific message circulars.
2. **Radio Physics, Modulation, and TDMA Link Layer (ITU-R):** The core physical layer, time division multiple access protocols, framing, message bit catalogs, and identity structures.
3. **Equipment Test Specifications and Type-Approval (IEC TC 80):** Pass/fail laboratory test specifications for Class A, Class B CS, Class B SO, base stations, AtoN, and locating transmitters.
4. **Digital Bridge and Network Interfaces (IEC 61162 / NMEA):** Serial interfaces (4,800 baud and 38,400 baud), CAN bus networking (NMEA 2000), Ethernet backbones (IEC 61162-450 Lightweight Ethernet), and cybersecurity gateways.
5. **Operational Guidance and Aids to Navigation (IALA):** System architecture recommendations, base station network planning, FATDMA slot reservations, and Aids to Navigation (AtoN) service guidelines.
6. **Locating Devices and Emergency Beacons (ETSI / RTCM):** Search and rescue locating transmitters (AIS-SART), personal Man Overboard (AIS-MOB) devices, and maritime survivor locating devices.
7. **Hydrographic Data and Electronic Navigational Charts (IHO):** Vector ENC standards (S-57), the universal hydrographic model (S-100), and dynamic navigational warning layers.
8. **Regional Waterways, National Transposition, and Adjacent Systems:** European inland navigation (CESNI ES-TRIN), US Federal regulations (FCC 47 CFR Part 80 and USCG 33 CFR Part 164), European Union directives (VTMIS and Marine Equipment Directive), and adjacent cellular positioning frameworks (3GPP).

## Standards hierarchy and reading guide

### The four-tier standards stack

The global governance of maritime electronics divides responsibilities across four distinct international bodies to preserve regulatory authority while enabling rapid technical evolution:

1. **Statutory Mandate (IMO):** The International Maritime Organization defines *who* must carry equipment, *when* it must operate, and *what* functional capabilities it must provide. IMO instruments do not contain schematic diagrams, circuit tolerances, or byte-level protocol definitions. Instead, IMO performance standards (such as Resolution MSC.74(69), Annex 3) state operational goals and explicitly incorporate technical recommendations published by the ITU-R.
2. **Radiocommunication Characteristics (ITU-R):** Working Party 5B of the ITU Radiocommunication Sector standardizes the radio waveform, channel assignments, modulation parameters, framing syntax, and link-layer medium access control. Recommendation ITU-R M.1371 is the core world standard defining 9,600 bit/s GMSK modulation, 26.67-millisecond TDMA slotting, SOTDMA/CSTDMA access protocols, and the bit definitions of Messages 1 through 28. Identity numbering is governed by Recommendation ITU-R M.585.
3. **Equipment Test Standards (IEC TC 80):** Technical Committee 80 of the International Electrotechnical Commission turns the high-level operational mandates of the IMO and the radio parameters of the ITU-R into *testable, pass/fail laboratory specifications*. Manufacturers submit transceivers to accredited test houses (such as BSH, TÜV, or DNV) to verify compliance against IEC 61993-2 (Class A), IEC 62287-1 (Class B CS), IEC 62287-2 (Class B SO), IEC 62320-1/2/3 (infrastructure), or IEC 61097-14 (AIS-SART).
4. **Operational Guidance and Interfacing (IALA, NMEA, RTCM):** The International Organization for Marine Aids to Navigation (IALA) provides shore authorities with deployment planning, base station siting, and VDL slot management guidance. The National Marine Electronics Association (NMEA) and the Radio Technical Commission for Maritime Services (RTCM) define digital instrument buses (NMEA 0183 serial encapsulation sentences and NMEA 2000 Parameter Group Numbers) and specialized application standards.

### Obtaining maritime standards

Access to maritime specifications is split between freely downloadable intergovernmental publications and paywalled industry standards:

- **Freely Accessible Public Standards:**
  - **ITU Recommendations and Reports:** All ITU-R Recommendations (including M.1371, M.585, M.2092, and the P-series propagation models) and ITU-R Reports are available as free PDF downloads directly from the ITU portal (`https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-M/en`). The complete four-volume set of the ITU Radio Regulations is also freely accessible.
  - **IMO Resolutions and Circulars:** Official IMO documents are accessible to the public with free account registration on the IMODOCS repository (`https://docs.imo.org`). In addition, the United States Coast Guard Navigation Center (NAVCEN) maintains a comprehensive, open public mirror of governing IMO AIS resolutions, installation circulars, and ASM guidelines (`https://www.navcen.uscg.gov/ais-references`).
  - **IALA Recommendations and Guidelines:** All current IALA standards, recommendations, and guidelines can be downloaded free of charge from the IALA documentation portal (`https://www.iala-aism.org`). Historical guidelines (such as G1028 and G1029) are available upon direct request to the IALA Secretariat.
  - **IHO Standards:** International Hydrographic Organization publications, including S-57, S-100, and product specifications S-121 through S-129, are freely downloadable from the IHO portal (`https://iho.int/en/standards-and-specifications`).
  - **ETSI Harmonised Standards:** European standards for locating devices (ETSI EN 303 098 and EN 303 132) are freely downloadable via the ETSI publications search portal (`https://www.etsi.org/standards`).
  - **Government Regulations:** The United States Code of Federal Regulations (33 CFR Part 164 and 47 CFR Part 80) is freely browsable on the eCFR portal (`https://www.ecfr.gov`). European Union directives and regulations (Directive 2002/59/EC and Directive 2014/90/EU) are available on EUR-Lex (`https://eur-lex.europa.eu`).
- **Commercial Paywalled Standards:**
  - **IEC Test Standards:** IEC standards published by TC 80 (such as IEC 61993-2, IEC 62287-1/2, IEC 62320-1/2, IEC 61162 series, and IEC 62288) are copyrighted commercial publications. They must be purchased directly from the IEC Webstore (`https://webstore.iec.ch`) or national standards organizations (such as ANSI, BSI, DIN, or SIS). Individual standards typically range from 150 to 350 Swiss Francs (CHF).
  - **NMEA Interface Specifications:** NMEA 0183, NMEA 2000, and OneNet are proprietary standards available solely through direct purchase from the National Marine Electronics Association (`https://www.nmea.org`). Single-organization licenses range from US$1,150 for member equipment manufacturers to US$7,500–$10,000 for non-member industrial developers.
  - **RTCM Standards:** RTCM publications (including RTCM 10160.0 for static data resets, 11901.2 for MSLD locating devices, and 12100.1 for ASM qualification) are available for commercial purchase via the RTCM store (`https://www.rtcm.org`).
