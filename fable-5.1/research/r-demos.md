# Dossier: Demonstration programs and testbeds (St. Lawrence Seaway, Tampa Bay PORTS, USCG RDC ASM trials, Right Whale AIS, EU e-Navigation projects, IALA testbeds, Korea SMART-Nav, Inland AIS/RIS, VDES/R-Mode, and why ASM uptake stayed low)

**Purpose.** This dossier collects verified facts, primary documents, and lessons from the AIS demonstration programs and testbeds that shaped how binary/application-specific messages (ASM) and e-Navigation services were tried in the field between roughly 2002 and 2024. It feeds PLAN Chapter 43 (demonstrations and testbeds, primary), plus Chapter 4 (VTS), Chapter 7 (whales and environmental protection), Chapter 23 (ASM/binary messages), Chapter 54 (tide/weather/hydro over AIS), Chapters 11/12 (history), and Chapter 69 (VDES). Research performed 5 October 2026; all URLs accessed that day unless noted. Facts not confirmed against a primary page are flagged "(verify)".

## Key questions

1. Which waterway first mandated AIS carriage in North America, under what regulation, and what Seaway-specific binary messages followed?
2. What exactly was demonstrated in Tampa Bay (NOAA PORTS data over AIS), when, by whom, and with what message formats?
3. What did the USCG Research & Development Center's "AIS Transmit" project produce (reports, test beds, message specs)?
4. How did the Right Whale AIS Project work (acoustic buoys → AIS binary message → bridge display), and how did it evolve into Whale Alert?
5. What were the EU e-Navigation testbeds (EfficienSea, ACCSEAS, MONALISA/MONALISA 2.0, STM Validation, EfficienSea2, SESAME Straits): years, leads, funding, AIS/ASM components, and legacy?
6. What is IALA's role in testbed reporting (G1107) and the ASM collection/register?
7. What did Korea's SMART-Navigation build, and did it use AIS/ASM or move to LTE-Maritime?
8. How did Inland AIS / River Information Services become binding law in Europe (Directive 2005/44/EC → Reg. 415/2007 → Reg. 2019/838) and what are DAC 200 FIs?
9. What have VDES and R-Mode trials shown (briefly; details in r-vdes.md)?
10. Why did ASM uptake stay low despite two decades of testbeds, and what hard evidence exists for that claim?

## Notes and quotes

**Seaway as the first mandate.** The Seaway rule predates the US domestic AIS carriage rule (33 CFR 164.46, effective 2003–2004) by months and the SOLAS V/19 phase-in for non-passenger ships. Because the Seaway also operated its own base-station network and Traffic Management System, it became the first North American waterway where shore-originated binary messages (lock orders, water levels, wind) were routinely transmitted to ships. Its spec revision history (Rev 4.0 in April 2002 "to harmonize with PAWSS") shows the USCG Ports and Waterways Safety System messages and the Seaway messages were designed together; the Rev 4.1 changes (reading-type bits, reserved-bit alignment) are exactly the kind of bit-level drift that later made decoders fragile. (verify by opening the PDF.)

**Tampa Bay.** The USCG RDC chose Tampa because it had the oldest PORTS (1991) and a VTS. The architecture — a scheduled fetcher pulling PORTS, a formatter building Message 8, a VTS-shared base station, and an NAIS receiver as the monitor — is the template nearly every later ASM testbed repeated. The ION 2009 paper is the citable primary source; the June 2009 RDC Phase 1 report is the project record but its CG-D number was not found in this pass.

> "The AIS Transmit Project was initiated in 2007 to research what navigational information is needed by AIS users, determine the best way to transmit it, and test these capabilities at test-bed sites." — paraphrase of RDC project description as republished by mycoordinates.org (verify exact wording against the ION paper).

**Right whales.** The RAP chain was: Cornell/WHOI auto-detection buoys along the Boston TSS → shore server → AIS Message 8 "whale notice" area message from a USCG/NAIS-connected base station → display on bridge (Class A MKD text or ECDIS overlay). Schwehr's 2008 RTCM SC121 slides and the 2009 OCEANS paper (McGillivary, Schwehr, Fall) are the citable primaries. The Whale Alert iPad app (2012) effectively replaced the AIS-only delivery path, moving the same content to cellular/Wi-Fi and reaching far more users — an early, documented example of ASM being out-competed by IP delivery.

**EU projects.** EfficienSea (DMA, 2009–2012) → ACCSEAS (North Sea, 2012–2015) → MONALISA/MONALISA 2.0 (SMA, 2010–2015) → STM Validation (SMA, 2015–2019, €43 M) and EfficienSea2 (DMA, 2015–2018, €11.46 M; Maritime Cloud → Maritime Connectivity Platform). The AIS-specific content narrowed over time: ACCSEAS still tried route exchange over AIS ASM; STM moved route exchange to IP (RTZ files over SeaSWIM/MCP). CORDIS and seatrafficmanagement.info were read directly; ACCSEAS/MONALISA numbers are from secondary summaries.

**IALA.** G1107 asks testbed managers to report design, interim and final results so the community can reuse them; the ASM collection is informational and "does not constitute an official endorsement" of DAC/FI assignments (search summary quoting the IALA ASM page; verify wording).

**Korea.** SMART-Navigation is the clearest case of a national e-Navigation program that *bypassed* AIS ASM for shore-to-ship data: it built a dedicated LTE-Maritime network (263 base stations, 621 transceivers, 100 km coverage) and delivers 29 services via terminals and apps. AIS remains the tracking layer.

**Inland AIS.** Unlike maritime ASM, DAC 200 FI 10 and FI 55 are mandatory, implemented inside the transponder, and legally specified (Reg. 2019/838 Annex). This is the one ASM family with near-universal uptake in its domain — because carriage, content, and display were all regulated together.

**Why ASM uptake stayed low (synthesis).** Evidence assembled here points to five causes: (1) SN.1/Circ.289 itself cautions that ASM be used only where there is a clear operational need because of VDL loading; (2) no carriage or display requirement for ASM on Class A/ECDIS (SN.1/Circ.290 is guidance only), so most bridges never showed them; (3) regional DAC fragmentation (316/366 Seaway, 366/367 US, 200 inland, 1 international) and bit-level revisions (Seaway Rev 4.0→4.1) broke decoders; (4) IP alternatives (Whale Alert 2012, STM/MCP, Korea LTE-M) delivered richer content with less friction; (5) VDES was positioned from ~2013 onward as the "proper" home for ASM, freezing investment in AIS-channel ASM. A quantitative measurement of ASM share in NAIS or EU feeds was **not** located in this pass (see Open questions).

## Recommended use by chapter

- **Ch. 43 (primary):** Use the testbed table and timeline; lead with the Seaway (first mandate + first routine shore ASM), then Tampa Bay as the RDC template, RAP as the environmental use-case, the EU chain (EfficienSea → STM) as the shift from ASM to IP, and Korea as the national bypass of ASM. Close with the five-cause "why ASM stayed low" synthesis, clearly labelled as synthesis until a traffic-share measurement is sourced.
