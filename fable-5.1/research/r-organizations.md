# Dossier: Key organizations in the AIS ecosystem

**Purpose.** This dossier identifies, for every body named in PLAN Chapter 14,
what it is (treaty body, UN agency, standards body, industry association,
national administration, NGO, lab), what it decides or publishes that touches
AIS, which committee or working group does the work, the document series to
cite, and the public web entry point. It also records the status changes that
matter for a 2026 handbook (IALA becoming an intergovernmental organization on
22 August 2024; IMO's 2014 sub-committee merger that created NCSR; GFW's 2017
spin-out as an independent nonprofit; IALA committee renaming ENAV → DTEC).
It feeds **Chapter 14** (Key organizations and what they do) and **Appendix
J** (Organizations directory) primarily, and supplies material to **Chapter
15** (how the standards fit together), **Chapter 16** (which body issues which
legal instrument), **Chapter 23** (ASM registries: IALA ASM Collection, USCG
DAC 367), **Chapter 41** (government collection networks and open data:
NAIS/Marine Cadastre, DMA, Kystverket/BarentsWatch, Fintraffic Digitraffic,
EMSA SafeSeaNet, EMODnet), **Chapter 43** (testbeds), **Chapter 46**
(government software), **Chapter 52** (IHO S-100 governance) and **Chapter
69** (VDES governance).

## Key questions

1. For each body: legal nature, founding date, seat, membership, AIS-relevant
   committee, AIS-relevant document series, website.
2. Which bodies have *binding* authority (IMO via SOLAS; ITU via the Radio
   Regulations; national regulators) versus *recognized-standard* authority
   (IEC, IALA, IHO, NMEA, RTCM, ETSI) versus *influence* (CIRM, NGOs, labs)?
3. How does a technical change propagate: ITU-R WP 5B → M.1371 → IMO NCSR/MSC
   → SOLAS/performance standards → IEC TC 80 test standards → national type
   approval → IALA guidance for shore/AtoN?
4. What changed with IALA's conversion to an IGO (22 Aug 2024) and the first
   General Assembly (Singapore, 18–21 Feb 2025)?
5. Which national administrations publish open AIS data, and under which
   programme names (NAIS/Marine Cadastre, DMA, Kystverket/BarentsWatch,
   Digitraffic, EMSA/EMODnet)?
6. Which NGOs and labs are primary sources for AIS research (GFW, SkyTruth,
   Oceana, Pew; UNH CCOM, DLR, FFI, MIT LL, NPS)?

## Notes and quotes

- **Who binds whom.** Only IMO (through SOLAS and performance standards made
  mandatory by reference) and ITU (through the Radio Regulations, which are a
  treaty) bind states directly. IEC, IALA, IHO, NMEA, RTCM and ETSI produce
  standards that become binding only when a regulator incorporates them
  (e.g., 47 CFR 80.231 incorporates IEC 62287-1; 33 CFR 164.46 incorporates
  NMEA 0400 and IMO circulars; the EU Marine Equipment Directive lists IEC
  test standards for the wheelmark). IALA's IGO status (2024) does not by
  itself make IALA documents binding; it changes who sits at the table.
- **The propagation chain (Chapter 15 figure).** ITU-R WP 5B writes the air
  interface (M.1371); IMO NCSR/MSC writes performance standards (MSC.74(69)
  Annex 3) and operational guidance (A.1106(29), SN circulars); IEC TC 80
  writes the test standards (61993-2 etc.) that type-approval labs use; IALA
  (DTEC/VTS/ARM) writes shore-station, AtoN and ASM guidance and keeps the ASM
  Collection; national administrations (USCG/FCC, EU notified bodies) issue
  approvals; NMEA/IEC 61162 define the wires. RTCM's SC 121 historically
  hosted US ASM development (RTCM 12100.1) in parallel with IALA.
- **IALA renaming.** Older documents cite "IALA Recommendation A-124" (AIS
  shore stations) and "A-126" (AIS AtoN); the current numbering scheme is
  R0124/R0126 for Recommendations and G1xxx for Guidelines. The IALA "AIS
  Volume 1 Part I/II" technical guidance was reorganized into G1082 and
  others. Cite by current id with the legacy id in parentheses, and expect
  renumbering again after the IGO transition (verify in r-standards-iala.md).
- **IMO committee history.** AIS was developed at NAV (performance standard
  1998) and COMSAR (radio aspects); since 2014 both strands sit in NCSR.
  Citations to "NAV 44" or "COMSAR" are historically correct for 1996–2013.
- **National layer (US).** USCG sets carriage/operation (33 CFR 164.46),
  runs NAIS and VTS, and reviews type-approval test reports; FCC certifies
  equipment and licenses stations; NOAA/BOEM publish Marine Cadastre; USACE
  (AISAP) and DOT Volpe (MSSIS/SeaVision) are data users/integrators; NOAA
  NMFS OLE uses AIS/VMS for enforcement; US Navy sets its own transmission
  policy (see r-law-us.md).
- **National layer (Europe).** EMSA operates SafeSeaNet (mandatory under
  2002/59/EC) and IMDatE; DG MOVE owns the directive; DG MARE owns the
  fisheries Control Regulation; national administrations (DMA, Kystverket,
  Sjöfartsverket, Traficom/Fintraffic, MCA) run shore networks and, in the
  Nordic cases, publish open feeds; HELCOM aggregates Baltic AIS for its
  member states (verify current HELCOM AIS data policy).
- **Asia-Pacific and others.** AMSA (Australia) runs a national AIS network
  and craft-tracking; MPA Singapore runs the Singapore Strait VTIS; Japan Coast
  Guard and MLIT; China MSA; Canadian Coast Guard and Transport Canada. Treat
  specific network names/sizes as (verify) until each agency page is read.
- **NGOs.** GFW's distinctive contribution is open, documented AIS-derived
  datasets (fishing effort, encounters, loitering, AIS disabling) with
  published methods (Kroodsma et al. 2018; Welch et al. 2022; Paolo et al.
  2024) — a primary source for Chapters 6, 47–49. SkyTruth (Bjorn Bergman)
  published early GNSS/AIS spoofing analyses (2017–2020).
- **Labs.** UNH CCOM (noaadata/libais lineage, right-whale ASM work), DLR
  (R-Mode Baltic, satellite AIS), FFI (AISSat-1, NorSat), MIT Lincoln
  Laboratory, Naval Postgraduate School — cite specific publications, not the
  institutions, in chapters.
