# Dossier: IMO instruments that define, mandate, and govern AIS

**Purpose.** This dossier collects what can be confirmed, as of October 2026,
about the International Maritime Organization (IMO) documents that created the
AIS carriage mandate and performance framework: the 1998 performance standard
(MSC.74(69) Annex 3), the SOLAS Chapter V revision of 2000 and the December 2002
Diplomatic Conference that accelerated carriage, the operational-use guidelines
(A.917(22) → A.956(23) → A.1106(29)), the installation circulars (SN/Circ.227,
SN.1/Circ.245), the binary/application-specific message circulars (SN/Circ.236,
SN.1/Circ.289, SN.1/Circ.290), the AIS AtoN policy (MSC.1/Circ.1473), annual
testing (MSC.1/Circ.1252, SOLAS V/18.9 via MSC.308(88)), VDL protection
(MSC.140(76) → MSC.347(91)), AIS-SART (MSC.246(83)), VTS (A.857(20) →
A.1158(32)), LRIT (V/19-1 via MSC.202(81)), VDR (MSC.333(90)), S-100 ECDIS
(MSC.530(106) and Rev.1), and the May 2026 VDES resolutions. It feeds
**Chapter 10** (Standardization 1996–2004), **Chapter 15** (The standards that
define AIS), **Chapter 16** (Laws and treaties), **Appendix B** (Standards
register), and supplies material to **Chapters 1, 3, 4, 8, 11, 20, 23, 36, 51,
52, 53, 54, 56, 63, 68, 69**.

## Key questions

1. Which IMO document is "the AIS performance standard", when was it adopted,
   and what does it actually require (modes, information content, reporting
   intervals, capacity, initialization time, security)?
2. What is the exact SOLAS carriage text (Chapter V, Regulation 19.2.4), what
   are the tonnage thresholds, and what was the phase-in schedule?
3. What did the December 2002 Diplomatic Conference change, and why is
   "31 December 2004" the date everyone remembers?
4. What do the operational-use guidelines say about switching AIS off, about
   AIS in collision avoidance, and about the inherent limitations of AIS?
5. Which circulars govern installation, annual testing, binary/ASM messages,
   AIS AtoN, and VDL protection? Which are revoked and by what?
6. How do IMO resolutions relate to ITU-R M.1371 and the IEC test standards?
7. What are the adjacent IMO instruments a handbook must cite for VTS, LRIT,
   VDR, AIS-SART, S-100 ECDIS, and VDES?
8. What is free to obtain, and where?

## Notes and quotes

**The 1998 standard is short.** Annex 3 of MSC.74(69) runs about four pages.
It defines *what* AIS must do, not *how*. Everything about GMSK, TDMA, slot
timing, message bit layouts, and channel plans is pushed to ITU-R ("The
technical characteristics of the AIS such as variable transmitter output
power, operating frequencies (dedicated internationally and selected
regionally), modulation, and antenna system should comply with the
appropriate ITU-R Recommendations." — §9). Tests are pushed to IEC. This is the
origin of the three-layer architecture (IMO performance → ITU-R technical → IEC
test) that Chapter 15 should explain.

**The 1998 reporting intervals are not the ones in service.** MSC.74(69)
Table 1 says 12 s at 0–14 kn and 4 s when changing course; M.1371 and
A.1106(29) Table 2 say 10 s and 3⅓ s. The 1998 table also has no "moored and
moving faster than 3 knots" row. A handbook pitfall: quoting the 1998 table as
"the IMO reporting intervals". Use A.1106(29) Table 2 / M.1371-5 for current
values and cite MSC.74(69) only as history.

**"2000 reports per minute."** The 1998 capacity requirement (§6.2) is the
policy ancestor of the 2,250 slots per minute per channel in M.1371 (4,500
across AIS 1 and AIS 2). Chapter 21 can use this as the opening line of the
slot-map discussion.

**"Destination and ETA (at masters discretion)."** The parenthetical in
MSC.74(69) §6.1.3 explains why destination/ETA are so unreliable in practice:
IMO never required them to be filled in accurately. Chapter 5 (trading
signals) and Chapter 47 (data quality) should quote this.

**Security clause in 1998.** §6.3 asked for "a security mechanism … to detect
disabling and to prevent unauthorised alteration of input or transmitted
data." Nothing in the sentence covers authentication of *received* data. This
is the honest historical answer to "why isn't AIS authenticated?" (Chapters 58,
64): the performance standard asked for tamper-evidence on the ship, not
message authentication on the link.

**SOLAS V/19.2.4.7 vs. A.1106(29) para 22.** The treaty text says ships "shall
maintain AIS in operation at all times except where international agreements,
rules or standards provide for the protection of navigational information."
The guideline adds the master's judgement: "If the master believes that the
continual operation of AIS might compromise the safety or security of his/her
ship or where security incidents are imminent, the AIS may be switched off."
Both must be quoted together in Chapters 8, 16 and 63; the second is a
*guideline* adopted "taking into account" the first, not an amendment to it.

**Why 31 December 2004.** The 2000 SOLAS revision (MSC.99(73)) gave cargo ships
of 300–50,000 GT on international voyages until their first safety-equipment
survey after 1 July 2007 (and domestic ships until 1 July 2008) (verify the
original 2000 sub-paragraph wording). After 11 September 2001, the Diplomatic
Conference on Maritime Security (9–13 December 2002), the same conference that
adopted SOLAS Chapter XI-2 and the ISPS Code, adopted Conference resolution 1
(12 December 2002) rewriting V/19.2.4.2.4 to "not later than the first safety
equipment survey after 1 July 2004 or by 31 December 2004, whichever occurs
earlier." The domestic-voyage date of 1 July 2008 was left unchanged. The
NAVCEN excerpt read in this session already shows the post-2002 text.

**Quote for Chapter 10 (SOLAS V/19.2.4.2.4):** "in the case of ships, other
than passenger ships and tankers, of 300 gross tonnage and upwards, but less
than 50,000 gross tonnage, not later than the first safety equipment survey
after 1 July 2004 or by 31 December 2004, whichever occurs earlier".

**Quote for Chapter 1 / 55 (A.1106(29) para 36):** "The accuracy of AIS
information received is only as good as the accuracy of the AIS information
transmitted."

**Quote for Chapter 55 (A.1106(29) para 37):** "Incorrect information about one
ship displayed on the bridge of another could be dangerously confusing."

**Quote for Chapter 68 (MSC.1/Circ.1473 §4.1.2):** AtoN providers "should take
special precaution to the primary purpose of AIS for collision avoidance".

**The ASM circular lineage.** SN/Circ.236 (2004) → trial of seven binary
messages for four years → NAV 55 evaluation (2009) → SN.1/Circ.289 (2 June
2010) with FIs 16–32 and a sunset of the trial FIs 11–15 on 1 January 2013. The
display counterpart is SN.1/Circ.290. There is **no** IMO circular numbered
MSC.1/Circ.1473 on ASM policy; 1473 is the AIS AtoN policy. The AIS AtoN policy
cites A.917(22) as the resolution that first introduced AIS AtoN as a "future
application".

**Annual testing.** MSC.1/Circ.1252 (2007) was voluntary guidance until
MSC.308(88) wrote V/18.9 into SOLAS (in force 1 July 2012). The test items in
Circ.1252 — static data check, sensor data, voyage data, RF power/frequency
measurement, on-air test with a VTS or test set — are exactly the checks that
catch the misconfiguration failure modes catalogued in Chapter 36.

**VDL protection.** MSC.140(76) was adopted at the same MSC session (December
2002) as the Diplomatic Conference; its successor MSC.347(91) is the IMO hook
that administrations use against non-type-approved transmitters (fishing-net
beacons, uncertified Class B units). Chapters 60, 65 and 68 should cite it as
the policy basis rather than inventing a prohibition.

**How IMO texts are obtained.** IMO resolutions and circulars are not sold
individually; they are available to registered users through IMODOCS, through
member administrations, and (for AIS specifically) through the USCG NAVCEN
mirror, which hosts official PDFs of MSC.74(69), A.917(22), A.956(23),
A.1106(29), SN/Circ.227/244/245, SN.1/Circ.289/290, MSC.1/Circ.1252/1473,
MSC.140(76), MSC.347(91) and the SOLAS V/18.9 and V/19 excerpts. The consolidated
SOLAS text is a paid IMO publication. Fair quotation of short passages is
standard practice in flag-state notices and class rules.
