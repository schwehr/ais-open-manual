# Dossier: IALA documents and institutional status relevant to AIS

**Purpose.** This dossier collects what can be confirmed, as of October 2026,
about IALA — the International Organization for Marine Aids to Navigation,
formerly the International Association of Marine Aids to Navigation and
Lighthouse Authorities — as the body that wrote most of the operational and
shore-side guidance for AIS: the (now legacy) AIS Volume 1 guidelines
G1028/G1029, the overview G1082, the shore-station/AIS-service recommendations
R0123/R0124 (A-123/A-124), the AIS AtoN recommendation R0126 (A-126) and its
guidelines (G1062, G1081, G1084, G1098), ASM guidance (G1095) and the IALA ASM
Collection, VDES guidance (G1117; G1139 withdrawn), the VTS Manual and the
V-103/C0103 VTS training courses, the S10xx standards series, and the 22 August
2024 transition from NGO to intergovernmental organization. It feeds
**Chapter 14** (Key organizations), **Chapter 15** (Standards narrative),
**Appendix B** (Standards register) and **Appendix J** (Organizations
directory), and supplies material to **Chapters 4, 12, 20, 23, 37, 43, 52, 53,
68, 69**.

## Key questions

1. What is IALA, when was it founded, who are its members, and when and how
   did it become an intergovernmental organization?
2. Which IALA committees produce AIS/VDES material, and how were they
   reorganized in 2023?
3. What are the current identifiers, titles and editions of the IALA
   documents that matter for AIS: AIS Volume 1 Parts I/II, the AIS overview,
   the AIS Service / shore-station recommendations, AIS AtoN recommendation
   and guidelines, ASM guidance and the ASM Collection, VDES guidelines, the
   VTS Manual, VTS training, satellite-AIS guidance?
4. Which of these are legacy/withdrawn and what replaced them?
5. What does IALA's document numbering mean (R0xxx vs R1xxx, G1xxx, S10xx,
   C0xxx; old A-/V-/O- prefixes; `urn:mrn:iala:pub:` identifiers)?
6. What technical content in the AtoN recommendation does the handbook need
   (AtoN station Types 1/2/3, Physical/Synthetic/Virtual, MMSI patterns,
   signal-level and coverage expectations)?
7. How do IALA documents relate to IMO circulars (which IMO texts IALA
   drafted) and to US regulation (33 CFR 164.46 cites the ASM Collection)?
8. What is free and where?

## Notes and quotes

**Naming.** IALA renumbered its documents around 2016–2017. Old prefixes
encoded the committee: A- (AtoN/AIS), V- (VTS), O- (Operations), E-
(Engineering). The new scheme is R0xxx/R1xxx for Recommendations, G1xxx for
Guidelines, S10xx for Standards, C0xxx for model courses; the old id is kept
in parentheses — "R0126 (A-126)", "R0124 (A-124)", "R0103 (V-103)". Every
current document also carries a Maritime Resource Name
(`urn:mrn:iala:pub:r0126:ed2.0`), the MRN scheme itself being G1143. Chapter 15
should explain this so readers can resolve older citations ("IALA A-124") to
current ones.

**IALA wrote much of what IMO adopted.** NAVCEN's description of SN/Circ.227:
"These 14 page guidelines, prepared by the International Association of
Lighthouse Authorities (IALA) and adopted by the International Maritime
Organization (IMO), contains guidelines for manufacturers, installers, yards,
suppliers and ship surveyors." R0126's preamble likewise notes "that work
carried out by IALA on shipborne automatic identification systems has
facilitated the development and adoption of a suite of technical and
operational publications by other bodies such as IMO, ITU, IHO and IEC",
and lists among them "ITU-R M.825-3 on the characteristics of a transponder
system using DSC techniques for use with VTS and ship-to-ship identification"
— a reminder (for Chapter 9) that the pre-AIS DSC transponder concept was an
IALA/ITU track before STDMA won.

**Quote for Chapter 20/68 (R0126 §2.2.1):** "The Type 1 AIS AtoN Station is a
transmit-only station, operating in FATDMA mode. Hence the slots used by the
Type 1 AIS AtoN Station need to be reserved by a national competent authority,
using Message 20, transmitted from an AIS station in the coverage area."

**Quote for Chapter 68 (R0126 §4.2.2.2):** "A Predicted Synthetic AIS AtoN does
not ensure the integrity of the Message 21, and therefore is not recommended
…"

**Quote for Chapter 69 (G1117 §1.3):** "New techniques providing 32 times the
raw data rates than those used for AIS are a core element of VDES."

**Quote for Chapter 24 (timing; R0126 §2.2.1, paraphrase):** a Type 1 AtoN
has no receiver and so cannot synchronize from the VDL; it must hold UTC
from its own GNSS (or an external source) to hit its FATDMA slots — this is
the concrete case of "AIS hardware without a full receiver" the PLAN asks
about. (verify the exact R0126/IEC 62320-2 wording on timing sources for
Type 1.)

**Coverage numbers for Chapter 29/32.** R0126's "5–10 NM floating / 10–25 NM
fixed, depending on height" and the −107 dBm threshold "measured at the
air-antenna interface of the user's receiver (IEC standard)" are the rare
published link-budget targets for AIS AtoN; the −107 dBm value matches the
IEC 61993-2 Class A sensitivity test level (to be confirmed in
r-standards-iec.md).

**The ASM Collection is a register, not a standard.** The collection's own
disclaimer (mirror) says it "is not an endorsement of any message, and is not
an authority for any DAC/FI combinations". The international set (DAC 001) is
fixed by IMO SN.1/Circ.289; regional sets (e.g., USCG DAC 367, St. Lawrence
Seaway DAC 316, Inland DAC 200) are registered by their owners. US law
(33 CFR 164.46(d)(4)) nevertheless gives the Collection legal effect by
permitting only ASMs that appear in it "for use in the United States or
Canada". Chapter 23 should cite this precisely.

**VDES.** G1117 is the stable overview; G1139 was the technical spec until ITU
absorbed it into M.2092-1, after which IALA withdrew G1139. The handbook
should cite M.2092-1/-2 for technical detail and G1117 for concept of
operations, and should not cite G1139 as current. G1117 also records the
regulatory choreography (1 Jan 2019 → postponed to 1 Jan 2024 by IMO
MSC.1/Circ.1460/Rev.2; 2030 voice vacate deadline under RR App. 18 footnote
w) that Chapter 69 needs.

**Legacy "AIS Volume 1".** The PLAN cites "IALA G1028/G1029 (verify ids)". The
ids are right but the documents are retired (Ed. 1.3/2004 and Ed. 1.1/2002)
and must be cited only as history (Chapters 10–11). For current operational
guidance cite G1082; for shore service design cite R0124 and its appendices;
for AtoN cite R0126.

**IGO transition.** The Convention text (adopted at the Kuala Lumpur
diplomatic conference, 2020 (verify date and venue)) entered into force on 22
August 2024. From that date the organization's legal name is "International
Organization for Marine Aids to Navigation" but it continues to use the
acronym IALA; existing documents remain valid and are being editorially
updated. Chapter 12 and 14 should phrase this as "became an intergovernmental
organization on 22 August 2024" and avoid "renamed to IOMAN" or similar
acronyms not used by the organization itself.

**Membership.** Three categories: National Members (AtoN authorities),
Associate Members (territories/organizations not qualifying as national
members), Industrial Members (manufacturers, service providers). Under the
Convention, states become parties; the member-category structure after 2024
should be re-checked before Chapter 14 is finalized (verify).
