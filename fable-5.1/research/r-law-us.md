# Dossier: United States law and regulation of AIS

**Purpose.** This dossier collects what can be confirmed, as of October 2026,
about the United States legal framework for AIS: the statutory mandate in the
Maritime Transportation Security Act of 2002 (now 46 U.S.C. § 70114), the
Coast Guard carriage and operating rule at 33 CFR 164.46 and its rulemaking
history (2003 interim rule, 2015 final rule, 2016 compliance date), the FCC
equipment and licensing rules in 47 CFR Part 80 (§§ 80.231, 80.275, 80.13 and
related), the OPA-90 roots of vessel tracking in Prince William Sound, FCC
enforcement against "AIS fishing net buoys", the FY2021 NDAA mandate and FCC
WT Docket 21-230, and how NAIS data reaches the public (Marine Cadastre,
NAVCEN historical data requests, FOIA, the 2015–2016 EPIC v. USCG FOIA case).
It feeds **Chapter 16** (Laws and treaties) primarily, and supplies material to
**Chapter 8** (security and national-security uses), **Chapter 10** (2002–2004
standardization and MTSA), **Chapter 12** (2015/2016 rule expansion),
**Chapter 13** (MMSI assignment in the US), **Chapter 17** (legal issues; FOIA
for NAIS), **Chapter 19** (privacy — EPIC/NAIS), **Chapter 33** (legal note on
hardware and transmission), **Chapter 41/46** (NAIS data access), **Chapter
63** (Navy AIS policy after 2017), **Chapter 65/68** (fishing-net AIS
enforcement), and **Appendix B** (standards and regulations register).

## Key questions

1. What statute requires AIS on US domestic (non-SOLAS) vessels, and what are
   its thresholds and exemptions?
2. What does 33 CFR 164.46 currently require: which vessels, Class A vs Class
   B, operating rules (continuous operation, silent periods, MMSI accuracy,
   text and ASM messaging), pilot plug, exceptions, prohibitions?
3. What is the rulemaking history: 2003 interim rule (VTS-area carriage), 2015
   final rule, delayed effective dates, 1 March 2016 installation deadline,
   later technical amendments?
4. How do the FCC rules (47 CFR Part 80) govern AIS equipment certification,
   the Coast Guard's role in type approval, Class B static-data programming,
   and ship-station licensing / MMSI assignment?
5. What did OPA-90 require for vessel tracking, and how did it feed the AIS
   story?
6. What has the FCC done about non-compliant "AIS fishing net buoys", and what
   did Congress direct in the FY2021 NDAA?
7. How is NAIS data released (Marine Cadastre, NAVCEN HDR, FOIA), and what did
   the EPIC FOIA litigation reveal?
8. What did the US Navy change about AIS transmission after the 2017
   collisions?
9. What legal lines matter for the handbook's Legal-note boxes: receive-only
   vs transmit, land-based transmission prohibition, portable AIS, MMSI
   programming?

## Notes and quotes

- **The statute is older than the rule it enables.** MTSA 2002 (signed 25 Nov
  2002) put the 65 ft / 26 ft-600 hp / passenger thresholds into statute; the
  Coast Guard implemented them in stages — a 2003 interim rule confined to
  SOLAS vessels and VTS areas, then a 2015 final rule for all navigable waters
  with a 1 March 2016 installation deadline. The 2015 rule took almost ten
  years (docket USCG-2005-21869 opened with a 2008 NPRM (verify)).
- **Three regulators, one device.** The Coast Guard (33 CFR 164.46) says who
  must carry AIS and how it must be operated; the FCC (47 CFR Part 80) says
  what equipment may be sold and who may transmit; the Coast Guard again
  (type-approval letter under 47 CFR 80.231/80.275 and 46 CFR 159) certifies
  that a model meets IEC 61993-2 / 62287-1 before the FCC will certify it.
  This is why US AIS devices carry both an FCC ID and a USCG approval number.
- **Quote (33 CFR 164.46(d)(2)(v)):** "...the continual operation of AIS and
  its associated devices ... at all times while the vessel is underway or at
  anchor, and, if moored, at least 15 minutes prior to getting underway;
  except when its operation would compromise the safety or security of the
  vessel or a security incident is imminent." — the domestic analogue of the
  SOLAS V/19.2.4.7 "master may switch off" clause; note the US rule adds a
  logging and reporting duty.
- **Quote (33 CFR 164.46(i)):** "...broadcasts from AIS Class A or B devices
  on aircraft, non-self propelled vessels or from land are prohibited." This
  is the one-line legal note for Chapter 33/42: receiving is unrestricted;
  transmitting Class A/B from shore is prohibited except by FCC-licensed
  maritime support stations; AIS AtoN and base stations fall under separate
  FCC authorizations (47 CFR 80 subparts for coast/AtoN stations (verify
  sections)).
- **Quote (47 CFR 80.231(b)):** "In no event shall the entry of static data
  into a Class B AIS device be performed by the user of the device or the
  licensee of a ship station using the device." In practice, US retail Class
  B units are shipped locked and programmed by the dealer — a frequent source
  of confusion for owners who buy abroad or second-hand (see Chapter 36
  failure modes: default/zero MMSI).
- **ASM rate limit.** The "no more than one ASM per minute" rule in
  § 164.46(d)(4) is a US-specific constraint relevant to Chapter 23.
- **NMEA 0400 alternative.** § 164.46(a) accepts NMEA Installation Standard
  0400-3.10 in lieu of IMO SN/Circ.227 and 245 — a rare case of a US rule
  citing an NMEA installation standard by edition.
- **NAIS and the public.** The Coast Guard does not publish raw NAIS; the
  public path is Marine Cadastre (NOAA/BOEM), which releases filtered,
  down-sampled historical data, and the NAVCEN Historical Data Request for
  other needs. FOIA has been used for *policy* records (EPIC 2015–16) rather
  than for bulk vessel data. EPIC's released documents include a NAIS
  "Information Sharing Policy" and a list of "NAIS Information Recipients" —
  useful primary material for Chapters 19 and 46.
- **EPIC quote (Ralph Naranjo, via EPIC page):** "a sailor's Good Samaritan
  effort to share location data will automatically enroll them in a data bank
  that tracks all of their movements."
- **Navy policy.** The 2017 change did not make Navy AIS transmission
  universal; it required transmission in TSSs and high-density areas, leaving
  discretion elsewhere. Warships remain outside 33 CFR 164.46 by § 164.01(c).

## Candidate figures and worked examples

- **Table: who must carry what in US waters.** Rows = vessel categories from
  § 164.46(b)(1)–(2) and (c); columns = Class A required / Class B permitted /
  conditions (VTS area, 14 kn, pilotage) / legal basis. Add a row for
  recreational vessels (voluntary; licensed by rule under 47 CFR 80.13).
- **Timeline figure (Chapter 10/12/16):** 1990 OPA-90 → 25 Nov 2002 MTSA →
  1 Jul 2003 interim rule → 31 Dec 2004 SOLAS backstop → 2008 NPRM (verify) →
  30 Jan 2015 final rule → 1 Mar 2016 installation deadline → 2018 FCC
  advisory → 2021 NDAA § 8416 / WT 21-230.
- **Flowchart: how a US AIS device gets to market.** Manufacturer test
  report (IEC 61993-2 or 62287-1) → USCG CG-ENG-4 letter → FCC equipment
  certification (FCC ID) → USCG type-approval number → dealer programs static
  data (Class B) → vessel's MMSI from FCC licence or approved agent.
- **Worked example (Legal note, Chapter 42):** a hobbyist in Maine builds a
  receive-only station: no FCC licence needed (receive-only); may not
  retransmit on AIS channels from land (§ 164.46(i)); feeding data to
  aggregators is lawful; a Class B transponder on a kayak on a trailer in a
  driveway is a land-based transmission and must be off.
- **Worked example (Chapter 13):** MMSI provenance in the US — FCC ULS ship
  station licence (MID 303/338/366–369) vs BoatUS-issued recreational MMSI
  (not registered with ITU MARS, hence "unknown" abroad) (verify which MIDs
  are used by the recreational agents).

## Recommended use by chapter

- **Chapter 16 (Laws and treaties):** core section on US law built from the
  statute (46 U.S.C. § 70114), the rule (33 CFR 164.46 paragraph by
  paragraph) and the FCC rules; include the "three regulators" explanation
  and the continuous-operation/silent-period clause side by side with SOLAS
  V/19.2.4.7.
- **Chapter 8 (Security uses):** MTSA 2002 as the post-9/11 origin of the
  domestic mandate; NAIS as the Maritime Domain Awareness sensor; Navy 2017
  policy change.
- **Chapter 10 (Standardization 1996–2004):** 2003 interim rule and the VTS
  compliance dates as the US implementation of the Dec 2002 SOLAS
  acceleration.
- **Chapter 12 (2015–present):** 2015 final rule, 1 March 2016 deadline, and
  the consequent jump in Class A/B counts on US waters.
- **Chapter 13 (MMSI):** 47 CFR 80.13 licensing by rule; agent-issued MMSIs;
  47 CFR 80.231(b) static-data programming restriction.
- **Chapter 17 (Legal issues):** FOIA for NAIS — EPIC v. USCG as the case
  file; what was released; what remains unpublished.
- **Chapter 19 (Privacy):** EPIC's framing (Jones concurrence, third-party
  doctrine), the absence of a NAIS-specific SORN/PIA as asserted in 2015, and
  the BoatUS/Naranjo recreational-boater concerns.
- **Chapter 23 (ASM):** one-ASM-per-minute limit and the IMO/IALA-collection
  allowlist in § 164.46(d)(4).
- **Chapter 33/42 (Hardware, home receiver) Legal note:** § 164.46(i)
  land-based transmission prohibition; FCC certification; receive-only is
  unregulated.
- **Chapter 36 (Failure modes):** Class B static data locked to installers;
  pilot-plug power receptacle requirement.
- **Chapter 41/46 (Providers; government software):** Marine Cadastre vs
  NAVCEN HDR vs FOIA; NAIS architecture facts from EPIC releases.
- **Chapter 63 (Military AIS):** § 164.01(c) warship exemption; 2017 SURFOR
  directive.
- **Chapter 65/68 (Hacks; special-purpose AIS):** FCC 2018 advisory, FY2021
  NDAA § 8416, WT Docket 21-230.
- **Appendix B:** register entries for 46 U.S.C. § 70114; 33 CFR 164.46; 47
  CFR 80.13/80.231/80.275/80.1101; OPA-90 § 5004; MTSA § 102.
