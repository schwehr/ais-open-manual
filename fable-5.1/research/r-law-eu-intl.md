# Dossier: EU and international law on AIS

**Purpose.** This dossier collects what can be confirmed, as of October 2026,
about the treaty and EU-law layer that governs AIS outside (and above) US
domestic law: SOLAS Chapter V regulation 19 (carriage and the "keep it on"
rule) and 19-1 (LRIT); the December 2002 SOLAS Conference that accelerated AIS
carriage alongside the ISPS Code; IMO Resolution A.1106(29) on operational
use; COLREGs Rules 5 and 7 and the "all available means / scanty information"
reading applied to AIS; UNCLOS provisions on flag-state duties, innocent
passage and coastal-state competence; the EU vessel-traffic-monitoring
directive 2002/59/EC with its amendments 2009/17/EC and 2011/15/EU (Article
6a and Annex II — the >15 m fishing-vessel AIS rule); the fisheries Control
Regulation 1224/2009 Article 10 and its 2023 overhaul; the Inland AIS
implementing regulation 2019/838; the FAO Port State Measures Agreement; the
OFAC/State/USCG maritime sanctions advisory of 14 May 2020; UN Security
Council DPRK sanctions and the Panel of Experts' AIS-manipulation findings;
China's 2021 data laws and the collapse of terrestrial AIS feeds; and the use
of AIS in IMO GHG inventories. It feeds **Chapter 16** (Laws and treaties)
primarily, and supplies material to **Chapter 6** (fisheries/IUU: EU >15 m
rule, PSMA, Control Regulation), **Chapter 8** (sanctions, DPRK, China 2021),
**Chapter 10** (SOLAS 2000/2002 history), **Chapter 11** (Inland AIS 2007),
**Chapter 17** (AIS-off as evidence; sanctions enforcement), **Chapter 19**
(data sovereignty/privacy arguments), **Chapter 53** (COLREGs and AIS in
training), **Chapter 55** (COLREGs in accident findings), **Chapter 59**
(spoofing for sanctions evasion), **Chapter 66** (LRIT, VMS), and **Appendix
B**.

## Key questions

1. Exactly which ships does SOLAS V/19.2.4 require to carry AIS, since when,
   and what does 19.2.4.7 say about keeping it on?
2. What did the December 2002 SOLAS Conference change about AIS dates, and
   how does that relate to the ISPS Code?
3. What does IMO A.1106(29) say about switching AIS off, logging and
   reporting, and which earlier resolutions did it supersede?
4. How do COLREGs Rules 5 and 7 bear on AIS — is AIS "all available means",
   and can AIS-derived CPA be "scanty information"?
5. What does UNCLOS say that matters for AIS: flag-state duties (Art. 94),
   innocent passage (Arts. 17–19), coastal-state laws on navigation safety
   (Art. 21), and the limit that coastal states cannot impose equipment
   standards beyond generally accepted international rules (Art. 21(2))?
6. What are the EU AIS obligations: 2002/59/EC Annex II (SOLAS-type ships on
   international and domestic voyages; exemptions), Article 6a (fishing
   vessels > 15 m, phased 2010–2014, "maintain in operation at all times"),
   and how 2009/17/EC and 2011/15/EU changed them?
7. What does the fisheries Control Regulation (1224/2009 Art. 10; 2023/2842)
   add, and how does AIS relate to VMS in EU fisheries control?
8. What governs Inland AIS in the EU (2019/838, replacing 415/2007)?
9. What does the PSMA require that touches AIS/vessel identity?
10. What do the US sanctions advisory (May 2020) and the UN DPRK Panel of
    Experts say about AIS manipulation, and what happened to the Panel in
    2024?
11. What happened to Chinese terrestrial AIS data in November 2021 and under
    which laws?
12. Where is AIS used in MARPOL/GHG regulatory science?

## Notes and quotes

- **Two thresholds, three regimes.** SOLAS sets 300 GT (international) /
  500 GT (domestic cargo) / all passenger ships. The EU (Annex II Part III)
  lowers the domestic cargo threshold to 300 GT and adds fishing vessels
  > 15 m (Art. 6a). The US (33 CFR 164.46) uses length (65 ft) rather than
  tonnage for domestic commercial vessels and allows Class B for some
  categories. Any chapter that says "AIS is mandatory above 300 GT" needs the
  qualifier "on international voyages under SOLAS".
- **"Protection of navigational information."** SOLAS V/19.2.4.7's exception
  is deliberately vague; A.1106(29) supplies the operational test (master's
  judgement of safety/security; log it; report it where in a mandatory
  reporting system). The EU fishing-vessel rule (Art. 6a) copies the
  "exceptional circumstances ... safety or security" formula. The US adds a
  duty to report to the COTP/VTC. These three texts belong side by side in
  Chapter 16 and are the legal basis for the "AIS off ≠ illegal per se"
  caution in Chapters 6, 8 and 59.
- **Quote (Art. 6a):** "Fishing vessels equipped with AIS shall maintain it
  in operation at all times." — note the EU uses "shall", not "should", for
  fishing vessels; SOLAS uses "shall maintain AIS in operation at all times"
  too, with the navigational-information exception.
- **COLREGs reading.** AIS is not named in the COLREGs. The standard
  interpretation (IMO A.1106(29); MAIB and P&I guidance) is that AIS is one of
  the "available means" in Rules 5 and 7(a), and that AIS-derived CPA/TCPA
  based on COG/SOG rather than systematic radar plotting can be "scanty
  information" under 7(c). Chapter 55 case files should quote the official
  report's own COLREGs analysis rather than this generalization.
- **UNCLOS.** No UNCLOS article mentions AIS. Relevant hooks: Art. 94 (flag
  state must ensure ships are equipped and that collision-avoidance
  communications/signals are maintained); Art. 21(1)(a) (coastal state may
  legislate on safety of navigation and traffic regulation in the territorial
  sea) with Art. 21(2) (but not on design/construction/manning/equipment
  unless giving effect to generally accepted international rules — which is
  why SOLAS V/19 is the vehicle); Art. 19 (innocent passage: switching off AIS
  is not in the Art. 19(2) list, so by itself it does not render passage
  non-innocent, though coastal states may require compliance with Art. 21
  laws). This is the legal frame for "can a coastal state demand AIS from a
  foreign ship in innocent passage?" — the US answers yes via 33 CFR
  164.02/164.46(c) for SOLAS ships.
- **Fisheries: AIS vs VMS.** EU VMS (Art. 9 of 1224/2009) is a confidential,
  satellite-polled system for ≥ 12 m vessels; AIS (Art. 10) is the open
  broadcast system for > 15 m vessels. Art. 10(3) lets control authorities
  cross-check AIS against VMS/logbooks — the legal basis for the "AIS gap"
  analyses in Chapter 6. The 2023 overhaul keeps both and extends tracking to
  the small-scale fleet.
- **Inland.** Inland AIS is a Class A derivative specified by CCNR and the EU
  (Reg. 415/2007 → 2019/838), with extra fields (ENI, convoy dimensions, blue
  sign). Carriage on the Rhine is a CCNR police-regulation matter (RPR) and in
  the EU a RIS Directive matter — do not describe it as a SOLAS requirement.
- **Sanctions.** The 2020 US advisory is guidance, not a regulation; its force
  comes from strict-liability sanctions exposure under IEEPA-based programs.
  The UN Panel reports are the best primary catalogue of real AIS spoofing
  techniques used by DPRK-linked tankers (false MMSI/IMO, name changes, dual
  transponders, dark STS). After April 2024 the public reporting function
  moved to the MSMT.
- **China 2021.** The drop was driven by provider caution under DSL/PIPL, not
  by a rule that names AIS. Chapters 8, 12 and 41 should describe it as a
  data-sovereignty event with a measurable effect on terrestrial coverage,
  citing VesselsValue's ~90 % estimate as an estimate.
- **GHG.** AIS is a regulatory-science input (GHG studies, STEAM-type
  inventories) rather than a MARPOL compliance mechanism; the Data Collection
  System and CII use reported fuel data, not AIS.

## Recommended use by chapter

- **Chapter 16:** the backbone — SOLAS V/19 (carriage + operation), the 2002
  Conference, A.1106(29), COLREGs 5/7, UNCLOS hooks, EU directive/regulation
  stack, PSMA, sanctions law; use the side-by-side "keep it on" figure and the
  threshold table.
- **Chapter 6:** Art. 6a and Annex II timetable; 1224/2009 Art. 10 and Art.
  10(3) cross-checking; 2023/2842; PSMA port-entry data; UN Panel/MSMT as
  evidence sources for IUU and sanctions-evasion patterns.
- **Chapter 8:** OFAC 2020 advisory; UNSCR 2397 and Panel findings; China
  2021 data laws and feed collapse; LRIT as the confidential state channel.
- **Chapter 10:** Dec 2000 revision, 1 July 2002, Dec 2002 acceleration;
  2002/59/EC as the EU response in the same year.
- **Chapter 11:** Inland AIS legal basis (415/2007 → 2019/838; CCNR).
- **Chapter 17:** "AIS off" as evidence — legal baseline is that switching off
  is permitted in defined circumstances with logging; prosecutions turn on
  absence of justification/log entries; sanctions cases rely on the 2020
  advisory's red flags.
- **Chapter 19:** EU Art. 6a/1224 as mandated public self-location of small
  commercial operators; China 2021 as a data-sovereignty precedent.
- **Chapter 53/55:** COLREGs Rules 5 and 7 text; A.1106(29) operational
  guidance; MAIB/P&I warnings about AIS-based collision avoidance.
- **Chapter 66:** LRIT (MSC.202(81)), VMS (1224/2009 Art. 9), PSMA — the
  non-broadcast tracking regimes.
- **Chapter 69:** note that SOLAS V/19 is where any future VDES carriage
  language lands (cross-reference r-vdes.md).
- **Appendix B:** register rows for every instrument in the sources table
  with adoption and in-force dates.
