# Dossier: Sources — GFW history article, the "2025 dark-ships video", and the Scranton Bloomberg manual

**Purpose.** Three sources are named by title in `PLAN.md` §6 as inputs that
must be read in full: the Global Fishing Watch history article (Cutlip 2017),
the YouTube video `2tuS1LLOcsI` ("2025 GFW, AIS and dark ships" per TASKS 1.2),
and the University of Scranton Bloomberg Terminal training manual. This dossier
records exactly what each source is, what it contains, what it can and cannot
support, and the primary sources that must back any claim taken from it. It
feeds **Chapter 1** (what AIS is; the three purposes), **Chapter 4** (VTS; New
Orleans), **Chapter 5** (commodity traders and finance; Bloomberg BMAP/MAP FISH),
**Chapter 6** (fisheries, IUU, dark fleets; the video), **Chapter 9–10**
(history; Arroyo interview) and **Chapter 41** (data providers). The companion
`r-history.md` holds the chronological facts; this file holds the source
critique.

## Notes and quotes

**GFW article — usable quotations (attribute to Cutlip 2017 / Arroyo).**

- "The maritime Automatic Identification System (AIS) is a radio communications
  system by which vessels continuously broadcast their identity and position over
  public airwaves using unencrypted VHS [sic] radio signals."
- "The intent, according [to] U.S. Coast Guard Program Analyst Jorge Arroyo, was
  to improve 'situational awareness' for navigators and provide tracking
  capabilities for shore based Vessel Traffic Services (VTS)—akin to Air Traffic
  Control."
- "The new system had to be autonomous, continuous and digital—something that
  could automatically communicate and portray a ship's location to other ships and
  to shore-based Vessel Traffic Services without the risk of human error."
- "'The British were testing a VHS [sic]-based tracking system for ships going in
  and out of the Dover Strait,' Arroyo says. 'The Panama Canal Commission was
  testing a UHF system, and Swedes were developing another protocol.'"
- "By 1998, the U.S. Coast Guard had embarked on a plan to modernize their entire
  vessel tracking service network which relied on voice radio communications and
  radar. New Orleans was designated as the first port to adopt a primarily
  AIS-based system."
- Closing: "For nearly two decades the Automatic Identification System has surely
  prevented many collisions and saved countless lives."

*Editorial note:* when quoting, render "VHS" as "VH[F]" or add "[sic]". The
article is a good *pointer* for the US origin story and the Arroyo interview but
is not a primary source for any date or threshold; every number it gives must be
re-cited to SOLAS, CFR, EUR-Lex or the underlying paper.

**Video — what can be said without a transcript.** The automated fetch returned
the page metadata, chapters and description but no transcript; the author of
this dossier has **not** watched the video. What is established: it is a
~29-minute Johnny Harris explainer (Nov 2025) co-produced with Christophe
Haubursin, using Outlaw Ocean footage and GFW data, with GFW scientists thanked.
Its public framing (description + chapter titles) covers: AIS as the baseline
view of ocean traffic; vessels "vanishing" by switching off AIS; the Chinese
distant-water fleet as "the biggest fishing fleet in history"; conditions aboard
"dark vessels" (labour abuse, per Urbina's reporting); "shining a light" via
satellite methods (presumably SAR/optical + AIS matching, i.e., the Paolo et al.
2024 approach). Press coverage of the same topic (AP, The New Yorker) and GFW's
own Galápagos analyses corroborate the general picture of Chinese squid-jigger
concentrations outside the Galápagos EEZ (2020 onwards).

*What the chapter must not do:* attribute specific numbers (fleet sizes, % of
vessels dark, hours of fishing) to "GFW" via this video. Pull numbers from Paolo
et al. 2024, Welch et al. 2022, Kroodsma et al. 2018 and GFW's published
Galápagos/Ecuador reports instead, and cite the video only as a cultural
indicator of how AIS "dark activity" entered mainstream understanding (5.7 M
views in 11 months).

**Scranton manual — characterization.** It is a 2016 student onboarding booklet
(sign-up screenshots, keyboard colour keys, F-key menus). Its value for ch. 5 is
pedagogical: it shows the *path* a finance student is taught — F9 Commodities →
GLCO prices → DES contract page → BMAP for infrastructure/weather layers → ECTR
for trade flows → SPLC for supply chains. It demonstrates that in 2016 an entry-
level Bloomberg curriculum presented BMAP as an energy-infrastructure/weather map
and did not teach ship tracking at all. Any statement that "the Bloomberg manual
documents AIS ship tracking" would be false. The quote to use is the BMAP
paragraph verbatim (p. 59).

**Bloomberg's own public description.** The strongest public, dated,
Bloomberg-side document found is the 24 Oct 2019 GFW/Bloomberg release
(`MAP FISH`), which (a) confirms AIS-derived vessel data lives inside the
Terminal's geospatial tooling and (b) gives Bloomberg's one-line lay definition
of AIS. For BMAP ship layers and AHOY, obtain a Bloomberg help page, product
sheet or a Bloomberg News article that names the function (e.g., Bloomberg
energy reporters frequently cite "ship-tracking data compiled by Bloomberg";
collect two or three dated examples for ch. 5) — (verify).
